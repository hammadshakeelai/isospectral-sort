Theoretical Foundations

4 Paradigms of Pure-Math Sorting

Each algorithm translates the problem of sorting into a fundamental physical or geometric domain.

Paradigm 01

Brockett Double-Bracket

Steepest ascent gradient flow of $\Phi(H) = \operatorname{Tr}(HN)$ on the adjoint orbit of $\mathfrak{so}(n)$. Converges to a sorted diagonal matrix via the Rearrangement Inequality.

$$\frac{dH}{dt} = [H, [H, N]]$$
Paradigm 02

Moser Toda Lattice

Integrable Hamiltonian mechanics of $n$ particles with exponential repulsive springs. As $t \to +\infty$, asymptotic velocities decouple into sorted eigenvalues.

$$\frac{dL}{dt} = [B, L]$$
Paradigm 03

Optimal Transport

1D Monge-Kantorovich transport under quadratic cost. Brenier's Theorem guarantees the optimal transport map is monotonic. Solved smoothly via Sinkhorn scaling.

$$\min_{P} \langle P, C \rangle - \varepsilon H(P)$$
Paradigm 04

Box-Ball Solitons

Ultradiscretization of the nonlinear KdV equation. Soliton waves collide elastically with speeds $v = L$, naturally sorting by height in discrete spacetime.

$$v(L) = L, \quad \Delta x = 2L_2$$

Pre-loaded Result: Brockett (1991) Theorem 3
By the classical Rearrangement Inequality (Hardy, Littlewood, and Pólya, 1934), the linear functional $\Phi(H) = \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_{\pi(i)} \mu_i$ achieves its unique global maximum when $\pi$ is the identity permutation. All other $(n! - 1)$ critical points are unstable saddle points with negative eigenvalues in their Hessian.

💡 The Analog & Photonic Computational Paradigm

While discrete algorithms like Quicksort require $O(n \log n)$ sequential CPU clock cycles, continuous dynamical systems are intended for **analog circuits, photonic crossbars, and quantum simulators**. In physical hardware, voltages, optical waves, and quantum states naturally execute matrix commutators and flow in $O(1)$ continuous physical time.
